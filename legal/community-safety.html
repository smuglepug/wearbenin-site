Community Safety Centre

Most deals on WearBenin are ordinary, friendly encounters between people who live in the same city. A small number are not, and they follow patterns we can describe in advance: where to meet in Benin City, how to handle money, the scam scripts to recognise, and how to report abuse.

1. Eight rules that prevent almost every problem

  1. Never pay before you see the item. No deposit, no transport money, no reservation fee for a stranger. If they will not let you look first, walk away.
  2. Meet in public, in daylight. A busy forecourt, a bank, a stadium car park, a shopping plaza - not a private home, unlit street or hotel room.
  3. Bring somebody for anything expensive, and tell a third person where you are going and who you are meeting.
  4. Do the deal in one place. Refuse let us go to my shop around the corner or come behind the filling station. Second locations are where setups happen.
  5. Inspect properly before money moves - size label, colour in daylight, stitching, lining, stains, smell, missing buttons, and whether it is the item in the photographs.
  6. Confirm payments in your own banking app, never from a screenshot or an alert SMS.
  7. Never share your bank PIN, card details, BVN, NIN or one-time codes with a vendor, a buyer, or anybody claiming to be WearBenin. We never ask for them.
  8. Keep the record: WhatsApp thread, transfer reference, rider's plate or number, photos of the item as delivered.

2. Where to meet in Benin City

Good meeting points have people around, security or cameras, and somewhere to check an item without being rushed. Vendors and buyers on WearBenin commonly use:

  • Ogbe Stadium (Samuel Ogbemudia Stadium) and Ring Road. The stadium forecourt on Ring Road is busy through the working day, easy for both sides to find, with security presence and passing traffic.
  • Bank premises on Ring Road, Sapele Road and Airport Road. If a large transfer is involved, do it in your own bank's branch or ATM gallery where staff know you and cameras are recording.
  • Busy filling stations on Ugbowo-Lagos Road, Sapele Road and Airport Road - at the forecourt, in daytime, not behind the tank farm.
  • Ugbowo, UNIBEN gate, Ekosodin, Uselu and Uwasota during term time, where there is constant foot traffic.
  • Shopping plazas and supermarkets on Akpakpava, Mission Road and Airport Road - air-conditioned, camera-covered, and a good place to check footwear or fabric in daylight.
  • New Benin and Oba Markets before 5pm for a follow-up visit to a vendor you already know; for a first meeting, the stadium car park or a bank is better.

Avoid private houses, uncompleted buildings, behind-market lanes, motor parks at night, and any location the other person insists on while refusing the public place you suggested. That refusal is information in itself.

3. If you are the vendor

Safety runs both ways - vendors are robbed, shortchanged and distracted too. Meet at your shop or a public place; do not invite a stranger into your home alone. Confirm the payment in your own banking app before you release the item: a buyer in a hurry to take goods before the alert comes is running the oldest trick there is. Watch for pairs and groups, where one talks, one handles the stock and a third waits at the gate - keep expensive pieces in your own hands. Count cash at a bank, not on the street. Do not let a buyer take an item home to test without full payment, do not hand over your phone to check the transfer, and do not read out a one-time code you receive while they are standing with you. Note the buyer's name, number and the plate details of whoever collects stock. If you are the victim of a set-up, report to the police station serving where it happened, then tell us so we can preserve evidence and remove the buyer.

4. Fraud warnings - the scripts to know

ScriptWhat it looks like
Fake credit alertAn SMS, screenshot or teller slip shows money has been sent. Nothing landed. Verify in your own app.
Check it laterThe network is bad, release the item now, confirm later. Refuse.
Wrong account nameYou agreed with Ada, the transfer screen shows another name. Stop and get the explanation in writing.
Overpayment reversalExtra money is sent and you are asked to refund the difference. The credit is fake or will be reversed.
Advance transport feePay a rider or delivery agent before any item exists. Pay on delivery only.
Fake WearBenin staffSomebody claims to be our escrow, verification or refund team and asks for money or codes. We have no escrow, we never ask for your card or OTP, and we never ask you to pay us to release anything.
Phishing linkA message links to a page that looks like our login. We only operate at wearbenin.ng.
Deposit for a bigger prizeA buyer abroad offers an inflated price, then asks you to pay a clearing or registration fee.

If money has already left your account, call your bank's customer care immediately and report it to the police station covering the transaction. Report it to us too, even if you do not expect to recover the money - one report protects the next person.

5. Reporting abuse, and what we do with a report

Three routes, in order of speed. Use the Report button on the listing, shop or message thread - it reaches our admin queue with a timestamp and the listing details attached. Email support@wearbenin.ng with the listing link, shop name, screenshots and your phone number if you want a call back. Or message WhatsApp +234 913 688 7631 if the matter is urgent, such as a fake listing collecting deposits right now, threats, or imagery involving a minor.

A person reviews every report, usually within 24 hours and always within 48 hours for safety matters, and we may hide the listing while we investigate. Where we find a breach we remove the content, warn, restrict or close the account and preserve the records. Where the conduct looks criminal - fraud, threats, stolen goods, sexual imagery of a real person, anything involving a child - we report it to the Nigeria Police Force and, for financial crime, the EFCC, and we cooperate with their requests for records.

What we cannot do: we are not a police force, we cannot arrest anybody, we cannot reverse a bank transfer, and we cannot recover money from a scammer's account. We can remove content, close accounts, keep evidence and hand it to the authorities who do have those powers. Related: Refunds & Disputes, Payments & Delivery Terms, Prohibited items.
