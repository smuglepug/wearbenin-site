Payments & Delivery Terms

WearBenin does not take payments. There is no card form, no checkout, no wallet, no escrow and no pay on WearBenin button, and there will be none until we say so on this page. Money moves directly between buyer and vendor, and the platform is never in the middle of it.

1. The current payment position

Buyers reach vendors by WhatsApp or phone from the listing page, agree a price, and settle directly. Two patterns cover almost everything in Benin City:

  • Meet in person and pay on the spot - cash, a transfer made in front of the vendor, or the vendor's POS terminal. This is the safest method and the one we recommend.
  • Pay on delivery - you agree the item and price in advance, a rider brings it, and you pay after you have opened the bag and looked at it.

Card details never touch our servers because we never ask for them. We do not accept, store or transmit card numbers, CVVs, PINs, account numbers, BVNs, NINs or one-time codes - not at registration, not in messages, nowhere. If a page or a chat claiming to be WearBenin asks for any of those, it is phishing: close it, screenshot it, send it to support@wearbenin.ng.

2. How to pay safely

  • See the item before you send a Naira. No exceptions for a stranger. A vendor who refuses inspection is not a vendor you should pay.
  • Treat a credit alert as unconfirmed until you see it in your own banking app. SMS alerts, screenshots and printouts are easy to fake. The network is slow, check it later is a scam script.
  • Match the account name to the person. If you agreed with Mama Vero and the transfer screen shows another name, stop and ask why in writing before you send.
  • Never pay a delivery or transport fee to a third party in advance. Agree rider fees in the chat and pay on delivery.
  • Refuse overpayment offers. If a buyer sends more than the price and asks for the difference back, that is the overpayment scam.
  • Do not hand your phone to anybody to confirm the transfer; that is how one-time codes and bank apps get hijacked.

3. Delivery across Benin City

WearBenin runs no delivery fleet. The vendor's rider, a dispatch rider either side hires, or your own pickup - that is how items move. The zones below are how vendors and riders actually talk about the city, so both sides understand each other. Give a landmark rather than a street number; many Benin City addresses are found that way.

4. Risk, inspection and handover

Risk in the goods passes to the buyer on handover - the moment you take the item and pay, or the moment a rider hands it to you and you accept it after inspection. After that point the buyer carries the risk of loss or damage, the vendor is not responsible for damage after handover, and a rider who damages or loses an item is an issue between whoever hired the rider and the rider.

Inspect before you accept. Open the bag, check the item against the photographs and the agreed description - size, colour, stains, stitching, smell. If it is not what was agreed you are entitled to refuse it and not pay; that is the point of pay-on-delivery. Accept without looking and you have accepted it.

Keep the details: the WhatsApp thread, the transfer reference, the rider's plate or phone number, and a photo of the item as delivered. That bundle is what the police, the FCCPC or a court will ask for, and it is what lets us act on a report. See Refunds & Disputes and the Safety Centre.

5. If we ever add payments

WearBenin may one day add a checkout, card processing or a payment-holding arrangement so buyers outside Benin City can pay safely. If that happens, and only with a licensed Nigerian payment provider behind it, we will publish the change here and in Fees & Commission at least 30 days in advance, naming the provider, the fees, the refund rules and the data-protection implications in the Privacy Policy. Until that notice appears, any claim that WearBenin is holding a buyer's money, offering escrow or issuing refunds from a platform account is false. Report it to support@wearbenin.ng or WhatsApp +234 913 688 7631, Benin City, Edo State, Nigeria.
