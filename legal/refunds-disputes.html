Returns, Refunds & Disputes

The short version: WearBenin never receives your money, so WearBenin cannot refund it. Refunds come from the vendor who was paid. What we can do is keep the record of what was agreed, remove bad listings, suspend people who cheat, and point you at the Nigerian routes that can compel a refund.

1. Agree the deal in writing before you pay

Five sentences on WhatsApp prevent nearly every dispute we see. Agree and keep in the chat: the exact price and what it includes; the condition, including every flaw; the size label and measurements, and that the item is the one photographed; where and when the handover happens and who pays the rider; and what happens if the item is not as described, with a deadline for complaining.

Then inspect before you pay - take the garment out of the bag in daylight, check stitching, lining, tags, smell, colour and fit. Once you take the item and pay, the deal is done and the risk is yours. Checking first is the cheapest consumer protection there is.

Nobody at WearBenin will ask you to send money to an escrow account or a WearBenin agent to make a refund happen. No such thing exists. Report any message claiming otherwise to support@wearbenin.ng and read the Safety Centre.

3. What Nigerian consumer law gives you

Nigeria has no universal 14-day cooling-off right for online purchases, so do not expect to return a correctly described dress simply because you changed your mind. What you do have:

  • The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018. It gives consumers rights to information, to be heard and to redress, and forbids unfair, misleading or deceptive practices. Describing polyester as silk, or a fake as original, can breach it, and complaints go to the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) through its published channels.
  • Ordinary contract law. A sale is a contract. If goods do not match the description agreed, or are not fit for the purpose the vendor knew you had, you can claim your money back or damages from the vendor.
  • Criminal exposure in the worst cases. False trade descriptions, counterfeits and obtaining money by false pretences can be prosecuted under the Merchandise Marks Act, the Trademarks Act and the Criminal Code, which applies in Edo State. A transfer record plus WhatsApp messages is usually enough for the police to act.
  • Data protection. If a vendor misused your personal data, your rights under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the route to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission are in the Privacy Policy.

4. Escalation path, step by step

  1. Speak to the vendor (day 0-2). Stay in the WearBenin inbox or WhatsApp so the record exists. State what was agreed, what you received, what you want, and a 48-hour deadline.
  2. Report on the platform (day 2-7). Use the Report button on the listing or vendor page, or email support@wearbenin.ng with the listing link, shop name, chat screenshots, photos of what arrived, the transfer reference and the rider's details. Ask for a dispute review.
  3. Our review (within 3 business days). A person reads it. We confirm and preserve the record, may remove the listing and restrict or ban the vendor, and tell you what we found. We will not pretend we can release funds we never held.
  4. Independent routes (day 7 onward). Complain to the FCCPC through its channels. If money was taken with no goods and you believe it was deliberate, report to the police station where the transaction happened and to the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission. Send us the reference numbers and we will hand over account records on a lawful request.
  5. Mediation and the courts. A court-connected mediation centre in Edo State, where one is available, is the cheapest next step. Otherwise your claim belongs in the courts of Edo State - the Magistrate's Court for small sums, or the High Court of Benin City Judicial Division for larger claims. Watch the clock: a simple contract claim is generally time-barred after about six years under the applicable limitation law, so confirm the position with your lawyer.
