IP & Takedown Policy

Vendors upload their own photographs, and a marketplace of photographs is a marketplace of copyright questions. This policy explains how intellectual property complaints work here: what to send, the response times we commit to, how a vendor challenges a removal, and what happens to repeat infringers.

1. What we act on

  • Copyright - your photographs, listing descriptions or shop text copied by somebody else. The Copyright Act 2022 protects original photographs as artistic works and gives owners the exclusive right to reproduce and communicate them publicly.
  • Trademarks and passing off - a brand name, logo or slogan used without permission, or a listing implying an affiliation that does not exist, enforceable under the Trademarks Act with extra criminal exposure for false trade descriptions under the Merchandise Marks Act.
  • Counterfeits - fake goods sold as originals. Brand owners and their agents can report these directly; you do not need a court order and you do not need to buy the item first. See also Prohibited & Restricted Items.
  • Image and personality rights - your own photograph or name used in a listing or profile without consent, including after a deal has ended.

Out of scope: quality and refund complaints (Refunds & Disputes), resale of genuine second-hand branded clothing, and disputes where neither side can show ownership of the right.

2. What a valid notice contains

Email support@wearbenin.ng with the subject IP TAKEDOWN and these nine points - they are what let us act in 48 hours instead of asking questions for a week:

  1. Your full legal name, postal address, email and a phone number we can reach you on.
  2. Whether you are the rights owner, an exclusive licensee or an authorised agent, and who you act for.
  3. The right infringed: copyright in identified photographs, a registered trademark, an unregistered brand name, a design right, or your image rights.
  4. For a trademark, the registration number and class; for copyright, evidence of authorship such as the original files, EXIF data or dated publication on your own page.
  5. The exact listing or shop URL on WearBenin. Somebody is using my photos cannot be actioned without a link.
  6. Screenshots of the offending page with the date visible, plus the same content on your own page for comparison.
  7. A short explanation of why the use is infringing - for example that the vendor is not you, not your agent and has no licence.
  8. Two statements: that you have a good-faith belief the use is unauthorised; and that the notice is accurate and you are the owner or authorised agent, acknowledging that knowingly false notices may expose you to claims from the vendor whose listing is removed.
  9. An electronic signature.

You need no lawyer and no template. If writing is difficult, message WhatsApp +234 913 688 7631 and we will help put it into a usable form.

4. Vendor counter-notice

If we removed your listing after a complaint and you believe you have the right to post it, email the same address with the subject IP COUNTER-NOTICE within 14 days of removal, giving your name, shop name, email and phone; the removed listing URL; your explanation of why the content is legitimate, with evidence such as your original photo files or a supplier invoice and receipt; your consent to the jurisdiction of the courts of Edo State for any dispute about the removal; a statement of good-faith belief that the removal was in error; and your signature.

On a valid counter-notice we send it to the complainant and give them 10 business days to tell us they have filed a court action. If they do not, we may restore the listing. If they do, it stays down until the dispute is resolved or we are ordered otherwise. Restoring content does not mean we decided who is right - it means the courts are the right venue, not our support desk.

Repeat infringers: three upheld complaints against one account means permanent closure. A single deliberate counterfeit operation, or an account built entirely on other people's photographs, is closed on first detection.

5. Our legal position, evidence and corrections

WearBenin is an intermediary: we host content users upload, we do not commission it, we cannot verify every photograph, and - since listings are free and we take no commission - we take no financial benefit from any particular listing. We remove or disable access expeditiously once we know about infringing material, we designate the support address as our point of contact for notices, and we do not protect infringers. Relevant Nigerian law includes the Copyright Act 2022, the Trademarks Act, the Merchandise Marks Act and the NITDA Code of Practice for Interactive Computer Service Platforms and Internet Intermediaries, whose expectations we aim to meet voluntarily even where a platform our size sits outside its largest-user thresholds.

Evidence. On a valid notice we preserve the removed listing, images and related messages for at least 90 days, longer if a court action or police report is notified. Tell us if you need preservation beyond that.

Corrections. If we removed a legitimate listing, reply to the removal email and we will re-examine within 2 business days. A complainant who repeatedly sends unfounded notices may have their notices declined without more evidence, and we will tell affected vendors the complaint was withdrawn.
